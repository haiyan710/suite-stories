# Bangkok's intensity outside, intimacy upstairs | Park Hyatt Bangkok

_Thailand's first Park Hyatt sits above the Central Embassy mall, in a figure-eight building by AL_A and Pi Design. Day two goes to teak houses, the river and Chinatown; day three to a slow spa afternoon and a three-star dinner._

For a first trip as a married couple, Bangkok has plenty to do and very little quiet. The hotel sits above the city, and that is where you come back to each night.

One reviewer notes that the ground floor holds only a concierge area; the hotel lobby is on the 10th floor, marked "L" in the lifts. Park Hyatt Bangkok sits above the Central Embassy mall, in the figure-eight building by AL_A and Pi Design whose aluminium-tiled skin incorporates Thai patterns. Bangkok Post reported in 2015 that it would be Thailand's first Park Hyatt. Travel sources describe a skywalk link to Phloen Chit BTS, so you can reach Siam and beyond without traffic or a taxi at rush hour.

## Day 2 · Old Siam, the river, and Chinatown after dark

**Morning · Teak and quiet.** Start with breakfast in the Embassy Room on the 9th floor, served 6 AM to 10:30 AM per reviewers. One guest describes Asian and Western options, eggs cooked to order, fresh coconuts daily and coffee brought to the table. Take it outside if the terrace is free; one reviewer ate there each morning. Then take the Skytrain from Phloen Chit toward Siam, or a car (about 20-35 minutes in morning rush hour), to the Jim Thompson House Museum, a short walk from the National Stadium. Six teak houses gathered from Central and Northern Thailand were reassembled as a canal-side home, and you see it on a guided tour. It is a romantic first look at old Siam for the two of you.

**Afternoon · The river.** A car to Sathorn pier takes about 15-30 minutes in daytime traffic. From there, take the Chao Phraya Express Tourist boat, which runs between Sathorn and Phra Athit piers with an English-speaking guide. Ride up to Phra Athit and back to Sathorn on the day pass, 180 baht (50 baht single) per a Bangkok travel guide; fares may have changed [TK: current fare]. It is usually less crowded than the regular orange-flag express, and the commentary points out places of interest along the water while the two of you watch the riverbanks pass.

**Evening · Chinatown, for two.** From Sathorn pier, a car takes about 20-40 minutes in evening rush hour to Potong, a century-plus-old Sino-Portuguese shophouse that was chef Pichaya "Pam" Soontornyanakij's family home and Chinese medicine shop. Time Out describes it as one Michelin star, spread over several floors joined by a small glass elevator [TK: 2026 Michelin status, hours and booking]. Afterwards, climb to Opium Bar upstairs for a nightcap, then take the car back (about 25-40 minutes in evening rush hour) to the quiet of your room.

## Day 3 · The slow day, and the toast that matters

**Morning · Behind the blackout curtain.** Sleep late. If you want a stretch, one guest says yoga mats are available in the room on request.

**Midday · Air and green, if you feel like it.** Lumphini Park is optional and easy: about 15-25 minutes by car in daytime traffic, or about 25 minutes on foot. The park covers 142 acres, donated to the city by King Rama VI in the 1920s. Walk or sit for as long as you like, then return by car in about 10-20 minutes or on foot in about 26.
